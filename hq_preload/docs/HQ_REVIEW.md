# HQ review — what changed and why

## Previous Amp20 prototype limitations

The Amp20 prototype was useful for architecture and stage-role experiments, but it was not a top-tier amp model yet:

- nonlinear stages used simple one-sample waveshapers at base sample rate;
- most filtering was first order;
- the tone stack/sag/NFB roles were not fully modelled;
- there was no anti-aliasing strategy around strong nonlinearities;
- tube-stage interaction was mostly feed-forward.

Those are major quality limits before any comparison with Helix-class modelling.

## HQ direction

### Nonlinear oversampling

Strong amp/pedal nonlinearities now run inside a 4x JUCE oversampling domain by default. This reduces fold-back aliasing generated by clipping and rectification. 2x can be selected later as an ECO mode and 8x is worth benchmarking for offline/reference rendering.

### Tube-stage identity

V1A, V1B, cold clipper, recovery, PI and power tube stages use different parameter sets and different asymmetry/memory. They are no longer meant to be interchangeable generic Drive blocks.

### Dynamic memory

A small state term is used in nonlinear tube stages so their operating point can move with recent signal energy instead of being perfectly memoryless.

### Sag

Sag is driven by smoothed power-stage demand and recovers over time. It changes the effective power-stage drive rather than acting as a static compressor after the amp.

### NFB / damping

NFB is routed from transformer output history back toward the power-stage input. This is deliberately different from treating damping as a final EQ/level control.

### Pedals

Pedals share utility code, not character. Pre-emphasis, nonlinear law and post filtering are chosen per model. Octave fuzz includes a dedicated rectifier/octave path; Velcro includes starvation/gating state; germanium and silicon fuzz use intentionally different asymmetry/threshold behaviour.

### Time effects

Chorus/flanger/vibrato and delay use fractional delay interpolation. Delay families change the feedback path itself: analog loses bandwidth and saturates; tape adds saturation plus wow/flutter; digital remains substantially cleaner.

### Reverb

The preload uses an 8-line feedback-delay network foundation. Room, plate, hall and spring select different delay lengths, damping/modulation and nonlinear/drip behaviour. They are not just four decay-time presets.

## Still required before "reference-class" claim

- measured anti-alias performance;
- triode/power-tube curve fitting against a reference circuit or SPICE/model data;
- improved tone-stack interaction model;
- speaker/cabinet convolution and impedance interaction;
- sample-rate/parameter stress tests;
- Pi CPU profiling;
- reamped level-matched blind comparison.

Source-code sophistication alone is not evidence that the sound is better.
